## About the channel

[“Serhii Babich and the Wonderful World of Web Development”](https://youtube.com/@babichweb) is a channel for developers and the people who work alongside them.

I care about more than code: how developers get through interviews, grow, change roles, work in teams, make decisions and mistakes, and cope with a changing industry.

Technical topics stay tied to real work. Frontend, the web platform, AI, hiring, careers, engineering levels, learning, and teamwork add up to one conversation about what it means to be a developer today.

</section>

<section>

## Who this is for

Companies [hiring engineers](/interviews/hiring) can show more than open roles: their people, how they evaluate engineers, how their teams work, and what development looks like inside the company.

Technical products and services can explain themselves through the problems developers actually have, not a walk through a feature list.

Courses, mentors, and educational projects can talk about learning, professional growth, and getting into IT, without promises of quick success or glossy stories about a “new life in three months.”

Conferences, communities, and events can reach Ukrainian developers through a channel they already trust on technical and professional topics.

</section>

## Partnership formats

### Partner episode

A standalone video backed by a partner.

It's built around a question the audience genuinely cares about: an interview, a career topic, learning, teamwork, tools, the market, AI, technical decisions, or professional growth.

The partner isn't just “mentioned.” They join the conversation through a topic, expertise, product, people, or experience that developers can use.

### Interview with a company expert

A conversation with an engineer, tech lead, founder, mentor, or anyone who knows how development really works inside your company.

The focus isn't how great the company is. It's experience: what you build, how you make decisions, how you hire, where you went wrong, what you've learned, and what others can take from it.

### Public interview or technical review

This format suits companies, educational projects, and teams that care about hiring, skills assessment, or preparing developers.

We can run a mock interview, review answers, break down common mistakes, discuss evaluation criteria, or make an episode about how technical interviews have changed.

### Live podcast or offline event

A format with an audience in the room. The company talks with developers directly, answers their questions, and becomes part of a live professional conversation instead of just appearing in a video.

It works when you want more than a video: people gathered around a topic, meeting the company in person.

### Custom format

We can build an idea around your goal: an interview on stage, “one question, three answers,” a technical discussion, a case review, or a conversation with several people from your team.

The point isn't to fill a ready-made ad template but to find a format where the company genuinely has something to say.

### Integration into a relevant episode

A short partner mention in a video where the product, event, or company naturally fits the topic.

I adapt the text to the audience, the channel's tone, and the episode. I don't read ready-made marketing copy that sounds foreign to the channel.

## How I work with partner content

I take a partnership when the partner, the topic, and the channel's viewers genuinely fit together.

I don't disguise advertising as a personal recommendation, take topics far from developers, or read marketing copy unedited. If an integration would damage the channel's trust, I turn it down.

Partner content should feel like part of the conversation, not a banner dropped into the video.

</section>

<section>

## What the partner gets

You reach Ukrainian developers in a format where you can explain a product, event, company, expertise, or hiring approach without sounding like a press release.

The content works beyond YouTube too: in social media, recruiting, communications, content marketing, or employer branding.

Most importantly, the episode doesn't turn into an ad break. Editorial independence keeps the audience's trust and makes your presence part of the conversation, not a foreign object in the middle of the video.

</section>

<section>

## When this is not a fit

This isn't the right format if you need a simple ad read or approved marketing copy that can't be meaningfully edited.

It also won't work if the product, event, or offer has no connection to developers, technology, education, the IT market, or professional growth.

And it's not a fit if you expect a guaranteed positive review. Editorial independence stays with me.

</section>
