## About the channel

[“Serhii Babich and the Wonderful World of Web Development”](https://youtube.com/@babichweb) is a channel for developers and people who work close to software development.

I'm interested in more than code: how developers go through interviews, grow professionally, change roles, work in teams, make mistakes and decisions, and deal with change in the industry.

Technical topics here are connected to real work. Frontend, the web platform, AI, hiring, careers, engineering levels, learning, and teamwork all become one conversation about what it means to be a developer today.

</section>

<section>

## Who this is for

It can be useful for companies [hiring engineers](/interviews/hiring) who want to show more than open roles: their people, their approach to technical evaluation, their teamwork, and how development actually works inside the company.

It also fits technical products and services that need to explain themselves through developer problems instead of just walking through a feature list.

Educational projects, courses, and mentors can talk here about learning, professional growth, and entering IT, without promises of fast success or glossy stories about a “new life in three months.”

Conferences, communities, and events can reach Ukrainian developers through a channel that already has trust around technical and professional topics.

</section>

## Partnership formats

### Partner episode

A standalone video supported by a partner.

The topic is built around a question the audience genuinely cares about. It can be an interview, a career topic, learning, teamwork, tools, the market, AI, technical decisions, or professional growth.

The partner isn't just “mentioned.” They join the conversation through a topic, expertise, product, people, or experience that can be useful to developers.

### Interview with a company expert

A conversation with an engineer, technical lead, founder, mentor, or someone who really understands how development works inside the company.

The focus isn't how great the company is. It's experience: what you build, how you make decisions, how you hire, where you made mistakes, what you've already learned, and what may be useful to others.

### Public interview or technical review

This format fits companies, educational projects, and teams that care about hiring, skill evaluation, or developer preparation.

We can do a mock interview, review answers, analyze common mistakes, discuss evaluation criteria, or make an episode about how technical interviews have changed.

### Live podcast or offline event

A format with an audience in the room. The company can speak directly with developers, answer questions, and become part of a live professional conversation instead of only appearing in a video.

This works when you want more than a video: people gathered around a topic, seeing the company live.

### Custom format

We can build a separate idea around your goal: for example, an interview on stage, “one question, three answers,” a technical discussion, a case review, or a conversation with several people from the team.

The point isn't to fit a ready-made advertising template but to find a format where the company genuinely has something to say.

### Integration into a relevant episode

A short partner mention in a video where the product, event, or company is naturally connected to the topic.

I adapt the text to the audience, the channel's tone, and the episode. I don't read ready-made marketing paragraphs that sound alien to it.

## How I work with partner content

I take on partnerships when I see a healthy connection between the partner, the topic, and the people who watch the channel.

I don't disguise advertising as a personal recommendation, take topics far from developers, or read ready-made marketing copy without edits. If an integration would damage trust in the channel, I don't take it.

Partner content should feel like part of the conversation, not a banner dropped into the video.

</section>

<section>

## What the partner gets

The partner gets access to Ukrainian developers and a format where they can explain a product, event, company, expertise, or hiring approach without a press-release tone.

This content can be used beyond YouTube. It can work in social media, recruiting, communications, content marketing, or employer branding.

Most importantly, the episode doesn't become an ad break. Editorial independence keeps the audience's trust and makes the partner's presence part of the conversation instead of a foreign piece in the middle of the video.

</section>

<section>

## When this is not a fit

This isn't the right format if you need a simple ad read or approved marketing copy without meaningful edits.

It also won't work if the product, event, or offer has no connection to developers, technology, education, the IT market, or professional growth.

And it's definitely not a fit if I'm expected to guarantee a positive review; editorial independence stays with me.

</section>
